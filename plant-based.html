Thomas's Chunky Guacamole with Tortilla Chips

Fresh, chunky and bright with lime. No cooking at all — just chop, mash and scoop.

Jump to recipe ↓
Prep15 minutes
Cookno cooking needed
Serves6 servings
Nutritionabout 160 cal · 2g protein per serving

Mash it chunky, not smooth — the texture is the whole point.

What you need

  • 4 ripe avocados
  • 2 limes, juiced (about 3 tablespoons)
  • ½ red onion, finely diced
  • 1 roma tomato, diced (scoop the seeds out first)
  • ¼ cup fresh cilantro, chopped
  • 1 jalapeño, minced (optional — skip for no heat)
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • ½ teaspoon garlic powder
  • tortilla chips for serving

Also grab: a cutting board, a sharp knife, a fork and a mixing bowl.

How to make it

  1. Pick ripe avocados.. They should give a little when you squeeze them gently, like a ripe peach. Too hard means not ready, too mushy means brown inside.
  2. Cut and pit them.. Cut each avocado in half lengthwise around the pit. Twist the halves apart. Whack the pit carefully with the knife blade and twist it out. Scoop the green flesh into the bowl with a spoon.
  3. Mash it chunky.. Mash with a fork until it is mostly broken up but still has chunks. Do not use a blender or food processor — chunky is what makes it good.
  4. Add everything else.. Stir in the lime juice, red onion, tomato, cilantro, jalapeño if using, salt and garlic powder.
  5. Taste and fix it.. Taste a chipful. Needs more zing? Add lime. Bland? Add a pinch more salt. This is your guac — make it how you like it.
  6. Serve right away.. Scoop it into a bowl, stick some chips around it and serve. Guacamole turns brown as it sits, so fresh is best.

How to serve it

With a big bowl of tortilla chips. Also amazing on tacos or toast.

Save the leftovers

Press plastic wrap right onto the surface of the guac to keep air out and refrigerate for up to 1 day. It may brown a little on top — just stir it.

Recipe 150 · Dinner

Thomas's Black Bean Tacos

Warm, filling tacos with seasoned black beans, crunchy cabbage, and cool avocado. Fast, cheap, and really good.

Jump to recipe ↓
Prep15 minutes
Cook10 minutes
Serves8 tacos
Nutritionabout 310 cal · 11g protein per 2 tacos

Warm the tortillas first — they bend without breaking.

What you need

  • 2 cans black beans (15 ounces each — one regular store can — drained and rinsed)
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon chili powder
  • 1 teaspoon cumin (a warm, earthy spice — find it with the other spices)
  • ½ teaspoon garlic powder
  • ½ teaspoon salt
  • 8 small corn tortillas
  • 1 cup shredded cabbage (a small handful per taco)
  • 1 tomato, diced
  • 1 avocado, sliced
  • 1 lime, cut into wedges

Also grab: a big pan, a can opener, and a cutting board.

How to make it

  1. Warm the beans. Pour the olive oil into a big pan over medium heat — that is the middle setting on the stove. Add the beans, chili powder, cumin, garlic powder, and salt. Stir and cook for about 5 minutes until the beans are hot all the way through. Mash a few beans with the spoon as you stir — it makes them thick and creamy.
  2. Warm the tortillas. Heat a dry pan with nothing in it over medium heat. Warm each tortilla for about 30 seconds on each side until it bends easily. Stack them on a plate and cover with a clean towel so they stay warm.
  3. Get the toppings ready. Shred the cabbage, dice the tomato, slice the avocado, and cut the lime into wedges.
  4. Build your tacos. Spoon beans into each tortilla, then add cabbage, tomato, and avocado on top. Squeeze lime over everything.
  5. Eat them right away. Tacos are best warm. Leftover beans keep in the fridge for 3 days — just warm them up in a pan.

Recipe 151 · Dinner

Thomas's Veggie Stir Fry with Rice

Crunchy veggies in a sweet-salty sauce over fluffy rice. One pan, one pot, dinner in about half an hour.

Jump to recipe ↓
Prep15 minutes
Cook20 minutes
Serves4 servings
Nutritionabout 340 cal · 8g protein per serving

Cut everything before you start cooking — stir fry moves fast.

What you need

  • 1 cup white rice (one regular coffee mug full, uncooked)
  • 2 cups water
  • 2 tablespoons vegetable oil
  • 2 cups broccoli florets (cut into small bite-size pieces)
  • 1 red bell pepper, sliced (cut off the top, scoop out the seeds, then slice)
  • 2 carrots, sliced thin
  • 1 cup snap peas
  • 3 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon maple syrup (or honey if you like it)
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1 teaspoon ground ginger (the dried spice, not fresh)
  • 1 tablespoon sesame seeds (optional — for sprinkling on top)

Also grab: a big pan or wok, a pot with a lid, and a cutting board.

How to make it

  1. Cook the rice first. Put the rice and water in a pot with a lid. Bring it to a boil, then turn the heat to low and put the lid on. Cook for 15 minutes, then turn off the heat and leave the lid on for 5 more minutes. Fluff it with a fork.
  2. Cut up the veggies. While the rice cooks, cut the broccoli into small pieces, slice the pepper and carrots, and pull the strings off the snap peas if they have any.
  3. Mix the sauce. Stir the soy sauce, maple syrup, garlic powder, and ginger together in a small bowl. That is your sauce — easy.
  4. Stir fry the veggies. Heat the oil in a big pan over high heat until it shimmers. Add the broccoli and carrots first and cook for 3 minutes, stirring a lot. Then add the pepper and snap peas and cook 2 more minutes. You want them bright and crunchy, not soft.
  5. Add the sauce. Pour the sauce over the veggies and stir for 1 minute until everything is shiny and coated.
  6. Serve it over rice. Spoon rice into bowls, pile the veggies on top, and sprinkle with sesame seeds if you have them.

Recipe 152 · Lunch

Thomas's Chickpea Salad Sandwich

Mashed chickpeas, crunchy celery, and a creamy lemon dressing on soft bread. Like a tuna sandwich, but no fish — and no cooking at all.

Jump to recipe ↓
Prep15 minutes
Cookno cooking needed
Serves4 sandwiches
Nutritionabout 380 cal · 12g protein per sandwich

Mash the chickpeas chunky — a few whole ones left in is perfect.

What you need

  • 1 can chickpeas (15 ounces — one regular store can, drained and rinsed)
  • 3 tablespoons vegan mayonnaise (the plant-based kind, in the same aisle as regular mayo)
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice (about half a lemon, squeezed)
  • 1 teaspoon Dijon mustard
  • 2 stalks celery, diced small
  • ¼ red onion, diced small
  • ½ teaspoon garlic powder
  • ½ teaspoon salt
  • ¼ teaspoon black pepper
  • 8 slices whole wheat bread
  • 4 leaves lettuce

Also grab: a can opener, a big bowl, a fork, and a knife.

How to make it

  1. Drain the chickpeas. Open the can, pour the chickpeas into a strainer, and rinse them under cold water. Shake off the extra water.
  2. Mash them. Put the chickpeas in a big bowl and mash them with a fork. You want them mostly squished but still chunky — leave a few whole ones in there.
  3. Mix in the creamy stuff. Stir in the vegan mayonnaise, lemon juice, and Dijon mustard until it looks like a thick salad.
  4. Add the crunch. Stir in the celery, red onion, garlic powder, salt, and pepper. Taste it — want more zing? Add a little more lemon juice.
  5. Build your sandwiches. Lay out 4 slices of bread. Put a lettuce leaf on each one, spoon the chickpea salad on top, and put the other 4 slices on top.
  6. Cut and serve. Cut each sandwich in half. They are ready to eat — no cooking needed.

Recipe 153 · Dinner

Thomas's Tomato Basil Pasta

Spaghetti in a fresh tomato sauce with lots of basil. Simple, fast, and tastes like summer.

Jump to recipe ↓
Prep10 minutes
Cook20 minutes
Serves4 servings
Nutritionabout 420 cal · 12g protein per serving

Save a cup of the pasta water — it makes the sauce silky.

What you need

  • 12 ounces spaghetti (one regular box is usually 16 ounces — use about three-quarters of it)
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced (chop it up small)
  • 1 can crushed tomatoes (28 ounces — one big store can)
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • ½ teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon sugar (just a little to cut the sourness)
  • 1 cup cherry tomatoes, halved
  • ½ cup fresh basil leaves (torn into small pieces)
  • 1 tablespoon balsamic vinegar (optional — makes the sauce richer)

Also grab: a big pot, a big pan, a colander, and a wooden spoon.

How to make it

  1. Start the pasta water. Fill a big pot with water and a big pinch of salt. Put it on high heat and bring it to a rolling boil — big bubbles all over.
  2. Cook the spaghetti. Add the spaghetti and stir so it does not stick. Cook for 9 to 11 minutes until it is tender but still has a little bite. Before you drain it, scoop out a mug of the starchy pasta water and set it aside.
  3. Start the sauce. While the pasta cooks, heat the olive oil in a big pan over medium heat. Add the garlic and cook for 30 seconds — just until it smells good, not brown.
  4. Add the tomatoes. Pour in the crushed tomatoes, salt, pepper, and sugar. Stir and let it bubble gently for 10 minutes. Stir in the cherry tomatoes for the last 2 minutes.
  5. Put it together. Drain the pasta and add it straight into the pan with the sauce. Toss it all together. If the sauce is too thick, add a splash of the pasta water you saved until it looks silky.
  6. Finish with basil. Turn off the heat and stir in the basil and the balsamic vinegar if you are using it. Serve right away in bowls.
