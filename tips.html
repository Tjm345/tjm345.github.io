Learn with me

Beginner kitchen tips.

I'm self-taught, so I learned all of this the hard way. Here's the stuff I wish someone told me when I started.

Tips that actually help

  • Read the whole recipe first. Know where you're going before you turn on the stove.
  • Get everything out before you start. Ingredients, pans, all of it. Cooks call this "mise en place" — I call it not panicking halfway through.
  • Taste as you go. That's how you learn what "needs a little more salt" actually means.
  • Don't walk away from a hot pan. Things go from perfect to burned fast.
  • It's okay to mess up. I've burned plenty. You just try again and it comes out better.
  • Clean as you go. Future you will be grateful.

Beginner's glossary

Words recipes throw around like everyone knows them. Here's what they actually mean:

1 lb (one pound)
One store package of meat, usually. That's all it means.
Skillet
A flat frying pan with low sides. If a recipe says skillet, grab your frying pan.
Preheat
Turn the oven on before you start cooking so it's hot when the food goes in.
Simmer
Tiny gentle bubbles, not a rolling boil. Low heat.
Dice / Chop / Mince
Cut into pieces. Dice = small cubes, chop = rough pieces, mince = really tiny.
Sauté
Cook quickly in a hot pan with a little oil, stirring a lot.
Fold in
Stir gently so you don't smash everything — like folding whipped cream into batter.
Rest
Let cooked meat sit a few minutes before cutting. It stays juicier.
Season to taste
Add salt/pepper, taste it, and decide if it needs more. Trust your mouth.
