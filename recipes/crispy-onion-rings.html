Thomas's Crispy Onion Rings

Thick-cut onions in a crunchy seasoned coating, fried golden. Way better than the frozen bag.

Jump to recipe ↓
Prep15 minutes
Cook20 minutes
Serves4 servings
Nutritionabout 340 cal · 6g protein per serving

Fry in small batches so the oil stays hot and the rings stay crispy.

What you need

  • 2 large yellow onions
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon paprika
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1 teaspoon salt, plus more for sprinkling
  • ½ teaspoon black pepper
  • 2 large eggs
  • ¼ cup milk
  • 2 cups panko breadcrumbs (Japanese breadcrumbs — extra crunchy)
  • 4 cups vegetable oil for frying (about half a bottle)
  • ranch or your favorite dipping sauce

Also grab: a deep heavy pot or Dutch oven, a slotted spoon, tongs, three shallow bowls and paper towels.

How to make it

  1. Slice the onions.. Peel the onions and cut them into rings about half an inch thick. Pop the rings apart with your fingers and pull out the tiny inner pieces — those are too small to coat.
  2. Heat the oil.. Pour the oil into the pot — it should be about 2 inches deep. Heat over medium-high until it reaches 350°F. No thermometer? Drop in a breadcrumb: it should sizzle hard right away but not burn.
  3. Set up your coating line.. Bowl 1: flour mixed with the paprika, garlic powder, salt and pepper. Bowl 2: eggs whisked with the milk. Bowl 3: panko breadcrumbs.
  4. Coat the rings.. Working one ring at a time: dredge in the seasoned flour, dunk in the egg wash, then press into the panko so it is fully covered. Set coated rings on a plate.
  5. Fry in small batches.. Carefully lower 4–5 rings into the hot oil with tongs. Fry 2–3 minutes, flipping once, until deep golden. Do not crowd the pot or they go soggy.
  6. Drain and salt.. Lift them out with the slotted spoon onto paper towels. Sprinkle with a little salt while they are still hot. Let the oil come back up to heat between batches.
  7. Serve hot.. Pile them up and serve right away with ranch or your favorite sauce.

How to serve it

Ranch, BBQ sauce or spicy mayo for dipping. Eat them hot — they lose their crunch as they cool.

Save the leftovers

Best eaten fresh. If you have extras, refrigerate for up to 2 days and reheat in a 400°F oven for 8 minutes to crisp them back up.
